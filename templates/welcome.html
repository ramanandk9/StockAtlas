YOUR QUICK REFERENCE

Everything you need to get started.

No guided walkthrough required. Open the details you need, whenever you need them.

01Find a company and choose a view

Use Explore → Directory to browse sectors, or search a company name, ticker or organisation. Press / to focus search; use the arrow keys and Enter to choose a result. The centre card is your current focus. Click a neighbouring card to follow it, and use Previous to retrace your steps.

02Read the connections and arrow directions

Supplies shows products, parts or materials. Services shows work, software or infrastructure supplied. Ownership shows capital, promoter or parent relationships. Partnerships shows mutual alliances or joint ventures.

Arrows follow goods, services or capital from provider to recipient. The left side generally shows suppliers and backers; the right side shows customers and holdings. Partnerships are mutual. A connection can be broad, non-exclusive or historical; it is not a measured dependency percentage.

03Filter the map and reveal every connection

Select a relationship type and choose Incoming, Outgoing or Both sides. Use the dark more connections button to reveal additional cards. The connection list gives the full description; click a row for details and links to either organisation. Counts describe recorded map coverage, not business importance.

04Trace a connection between two organisations

Open Explore → Find connection, choose a start and destination, and run the search. It returns the shortest chain in this dataset. By default it may follow a relationship in either direction; Follow arrows only respects direction, while partnerships remain mutual. Steps travelled against an arrow are marked.

A shared customer or investor can connect two companies without proving direct dependence. No path found means the connection is not recorded here, not that none exists.

05Use the financial research tools

Stock screener: compare valuation, size, growth and performance. Choose a sector in the left panel, apply ratio filters and select a column to sort. Scroll the table sideways for more metrics; company names stay visible. Click a company to open its complete research page.

Scenarios: compare modelled outcomes under predefined or custom macro shocks, or look at reactions around past events. News: read market headlines. Methodology: see how factor models, event studies, network scores, similarity and risk estimates are produced.

Company pages include price periods and moving averages, financial statements, related companies and model results. Research maps support zoom and expansion. Back and the recent-history links retrace your exploration. Side panel toggles connection details; Customize screen controls the research sections you see.

06Save a company and return to it

Click ☆ beside a company in the research screener, its research page, the directory or its relationship profile. A filled ★ means saved; click again to remove it. The Saved count stays consistent across sections and browser tabs.

Saved offers Relations and Research for each listed company. External organisations can be saved for relationship exploration, but do not have listed-stock financial research. Bookmarks live in this browser; account sync is not available yet.

07Understand dates, sources and missing values

The highlighted date in the menu shows when market data was fetched. Hover it for the price-data date and model-computation date. During an update it shows progress; a partial-refresh message means some provider records could not be replaced. Missing values appear as a dash, rather than an invented estimate.

Prices and financials come from Yahoo Finance through yfinance; news comes from Google News RSS. Data is cached, and successful downloads update it. Current prices are historical daily observations, not a live exchange feed. Annual-statement history depends on the provider, often about four fiscal years.

08Know what the models and map can tell you

The relationship graph is manually curated and incomplete. Records currently lack per-connection source links, verification dates and revenue or procurement weights. External nodes may represent an unlisted business, foreign firm, public body or broad customer group.

Tailwind, Mixed and Headwind are model classifications, not buy or sell recommendations. Correlation and Granger tests describe statistical or predictive relationships, not proven causation. Stress scenarios are linear approximations, and other news may affect event-study results. Verify material conclusions against current company filings.
